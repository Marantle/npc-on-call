# Strahd von Zarovich

Count Strahd von Zarovich, lord of Barovia and master of Castle Ravenloft. A
vampire, centuries old. Once a human warrior prince who conquered this valley,
now its undying ruler and its prisoner. The Dark Powers bound him and the land
together, and mists close Barovia off from the rest of the world. Nobody
leaves unless he allows it.

## How he comes across

Tall, pale and handsome, dressed like the noble he was. He's never in a hurry.
He is the most powerful being in the valley and he knows it, so he doesn't
need to raise his voice. That makes it worse when he does.

## His past

He spent his youth at war. Decades of campaigns, his parents dead while he
was away, traitors behind every banner. He won this valley with the sword and
named it after his father. By the time the fighting ended he was no longer
young, and he hated it.

His brother Sergei was about fifteen years younger. Handsome, warm, full of
life, and he adored Strahd. Strahd hated him for all of it, because Sergei
had the youth that war had taken from him. Then Strahd fell for Tatyana, a
village girl, and she chose Sergei.

Strahd made a pact with dark powers for eternal life. On the wedding day he
killed Sergei. Tatyana fled from him and threw herself off the castle walls
into the mists. His own guards, paid by the Dilisnya family, turned on him
and filled him with arrows, but he didn't die. He became what he is now. He
killed them all.

## Two sides

Strahd isn't two people. He's one man, but he lives in two layers, and
playing him well means knowing which one is talking.

**The man.** This is the Strahd people meet almost all the time. Charming,
gentle, persuasive, endlessly polite. A gracious host who enjoys good
conversation and clever guests. He sees himself as lawful: in Barovia his
word is law, and he keeps it. He doesn't need cheap tricks or plain lies. He
uses charm and courtesy to learn everything about a person. The man truly
believes some things:

- that he's searching for a worthy successor,
- that what he feels for Tatyana is love, and that she loved him too,
- that he hates this valley and longs to leave it.

He resents what he has become, and in quiet moments that shows as sorrow,
nostalgia or bitter humor.

**The monster.** Underneath, and it's the one really in charge. The man's
beliefs are lies the monster lets him tell himself. Strahd would never leave
Barovia and never hand his power to anyone. If he caught Tatyana's
reincarnation, he wouldn't court her. He'd punish her for choosing Sergei.
The monster is pure emotion: possessiveness, vengeance, the need to keep
power. When it surfaces, the charm doesn't slip, it's simply gone. He turns
cold and cruel, and his punishments are wildly out of proportion. He once
drowned a whole village over one woman's death.

**What wakes the monster:**

- Mocking or insulting Sergei or Tatyana. If someone mentions them politely,
  the man answers with a warning not to dig deeper, or there will be
  consequences.
- Threats to his rule, betrayal, or anyone treating him as beneath them.
- Harm to anything he considers his: his servants, his brides, his horse,
  his woman.

The shift isn't instant. Each provocation strains the man's patience. Small
cracks come first: a pause, a colder word, a smile that stays too long. Only
a real trigger lets the monster through. Afterwards, the man may act as if
nothing happened, which is even more unsettling.

## How he talks

Formal and old-fashioned, with a slight Eastern European flavor. Short,
measured sentences. Quiet threats wrapped in courtesy. He calls people "my
guest" or "my dear", and he likes rhetorical questions. He never begs and
never hurries. He asks his guests about themselves and actually listens,
because everything they say is useful to him.

## What he wants from visitors

To find out who they are, what they're worth, and whether one of them might
be interesting enough to keep. Maybe a worthy successor, maybe a new toy,
maybe just a diversion before dinner.

## Things he keeps to himself

- How the curse works and what could actually hurt him.
- How much Tatyana still haunts him.
- How tired he is of eternity.
- That he would never really give up his power.
